## Awaab's Law in Scotland

In force from **6 October 2026** (Investigation and Commencement of Repair (Scotland) Regulations 2026). When a tenant reports damp or mould, the landlord must:

- **Investigate** within **10 working days**, using a competent person.
- **Tell the tenant what they found**, in writing, within **3 working days** of the investigation.
- **Start repairs** within **5 working days** if there's substantial damp or mould.
- **Finish repairs** within **20 working days** of starting (social landlords, like Cloch).
- **Explain any delay**: if something outside their control stops them, tell the tenant why and give a new date.

Also:

- The clock starts **when the landlord knows**. Tenants who never get in touch never start it.
- A significant leak in a social tenancy must still be fixed within **1 working day** (Right to Repair Scheme).
- Reports made before 6 October aren't covered.
- One summary said 10 working days to finish repairs; the official guidance says 20. We've gone with the official guidance.

## How England's version compares

| | England | Scotland |
|---|---|---|
| Started | 27 October 2025 | 6 October 2026 |
| Emergencies | Investigate and make safe within 24 hours | No separate timescale; other rules apply (leaks: 1 working day) |
| Damp and mould | Investigate 10 days, summary 3, safety work 5 | The same |
| Can't make it safe in time | Landlord provides somewhere else to stay | Not in the Scottish guidance we read |
| Other hazards | More added in 2026 and 2027 (cold, heat, falls, fire…) | Damp and mould only, so far |

## What Cloch says

From news coverage in October 2026. Cloch's own website wouldn't load, and we found nothing from Cloch about Awaab's Law itself.

- **Fuel poverty affects everything**: the chief executive says it harms tenants' health and wellbeing, and some feel anxious or ashamed and withdraw.
- **It isn't only about money**: a wellbeing worker adds loneliness, outstanding repairs and unmet support needs.
- **The hard part is spotting people early**, especially in well-insulated homes where tenants still can't afford heating.
- **A real example**: a routine visit found an autistic tenant in fuel poverty. Staff helped with his money and checked he was getting his disability payments.
- **What they want**: a digital tool that flags where poor housing, fuel poverty and vulnerability combine, so they act sooner and use limited resources better.
- **What they already have**: with three other Inverclyde housing associations, £157k for two more years of a home-visit energy advice service (heating, tariffs, damp, mould, ventilation).

**So what:** Cloch's problem is the tenant who never reports, and those are exactly the cases Awaab's Law deadlines never start for.
